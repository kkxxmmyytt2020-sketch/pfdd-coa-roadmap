# MAH, COI, COU (G3 §II.B, §III.A–B, §III.D; Walton 2015; Powers 2017)

## Treatment benefit and the MAH
- **Treatment benefit / clinical benefit:** a favorable effect on a *meaningful* aspect of how a
  patient feels or functions *in their usual life*, or survives (Walton; G3 §II.B; BEST).
  "Meaningful" = something the patient cares about and wants to not worsen, improve, or prevent.
  "In their life" = an effect only on a clinic-only task with no known relation to daily life is
  not a benefit — unless that relation is established (e.g., lung function in asthma).
- **MAH:** some aspect of feeling or functioning in daily life that is important to patients.
  Narrow (nausea intensity) or broad (lower-limb-related function). Often an abstraction over a
  group of related activities (Walton's "upper-limb-dependent personal activities",
  "ambulatory performance"). Complex MAHs are hierarchical: specific activities → lower-order MAHs
  → higher-order MAH (G3 Fig. 2: hygiene, continence, dressing, feeding, mobility → ADLs).
- Each distinct MAH needs a distinct COA and endpoint; an overarching "disability of disease X"
  still requires identifying its separate components (Walton).

### MAH selection criteria (G3 §II.B.2, §III.B; Walton)
| Criterion | Question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Important to patients | Did patients raise it; how important/bothersome? | G2 data, PFDD reports |
| Part of typical life | Is it an everyday feeling/function, not a clinic-only phenomenon? | G2 |
| Core vs distal | Proximal manifestation of the disease, or downstream impact? Prefer core; distal may be secondary (G3 endometriosis example) | Disease model |
| Plausibly affected | Consistent with mechanism of action? | Nonclinical/early data |
| Can change in the trial | Detectable meaningful change in the planned time frame? | Natural history, phase 2 |
| Breadth | Relevant to almost all patients vs some (affects endpoint strategy, G4 §II.A.2.e) | G2 subgroup data |

## COI
- The COI is what the COA specifically measures to help understand how the product affects the
  MAH (G3 §II.B.2). The intent may be to improve, delay/avoid worsening, prevent onset, or restore.
  Harms can also be COIs.
- **Direct:** COI = MAH (most PROs of symptoms). "Direct" means intended to measure the MAH —
  not error-free (G3 fn 20).
- **Indirect:** the MAH is deconstructed into simpler bodily abilities thought to underlie it and
  easier to measure (leg strength, walking speed, short-term memory) (Walton Fig. 2). One MAH may
  have several COIs, each covering part of it (G3 Fig. 3); none may be sufficient alone.
- **Continuum of indirectness** (Walton attribute 3; Powers Fig. 1): in-clinic simulations of daily
  activities and visual acuity sit close to the MAH; supine quadriceps strength sits far; biomarkers
  farther. The degree of indirectness sets how much evidence is needed that COI scores relate to
  the MAH (component H).
- Same MAH, different COIs by population: pain intensity (PRO) in adolescents vs observable pain
  behaviours (ObsRO) in children ≤ 2 years (G3 §III).
- A pain MAH has several facets (intensity, duration, frequency, quality); pick the facet that
  matters in this disease (Walton).
- Diagnostic signs are often poor outcome COIs (tympanic-membrane bulging in otitis media; chest
  x-ray in pneumonia — Powers).
- Powers: justify the COI → benefit relationship with empirical evidence; physiological plausibility
  alone is not enough.

## COU (G3 §II.B.3; Walton; Powers Table 3)
Specify: **target population** (disease definition, entry criteria, baseline severity,
comorbidities, demographics, cultures, expected events such as starting an assistive device) ·
**use of the COA** (trial objective; how scores form an endpoint, e.g., mean score at week 12) ·
**implementation** (setting, mode, who completes) · **trial design** (comparator, who is masked) ·
**schedule** · endpoint positioning (primary/secondary; regulatory role) · standard concomitant care ·
culture/language/geography. Changing any element can change measurement properties; re-evaluate
when the COU changes (Walton; a COA fine in mild disease may floor/ceiling in severe disease).
