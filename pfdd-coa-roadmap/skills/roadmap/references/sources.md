# Sources behind the workflow

All stage guidance is distilled from these seven documents. Cite them by the short key.

| Key | Document | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** | FDA PFDD Guidance 1, *Collecting Comprehensive and Representative Input* (June 2020) | From whom to get input and how: research objectives, target population, who reports, sampling (Table 2), representativeness (Fig. 2), saturation-based sample size, existing data, data management; glossary (App. 2) |
| **G2** | FDA PFDD Guidance 2, *Methods to Identify What Is Important to Patients* (Feb 2022) | What to ask and how: interviews/focus groups, non-leading questions, survey item rules, mixed methods, barriers to self-report, social media; study materials (App. 3), coding & saturation (App. 4), screening/exit interviews (App. 5) |
| **G3** | FDA PFDD Guidance 3, *Selecting, Developing, or Modifying Fit-for-Purpose COAs* (Oct 2025) | MAH, COI, COU (§II.B); fit-for-purpose (§II.C); Roadmap (§III: disease → benefits → measure → conceptual framework, Table 1); evidence rationale components A–H (§IV, Table 2); COA types (App. A–D); rationale table (App. E) |
| **G4** | FDA PFDD Guidance 4 (draft), *Incorporating COAs Into Endpoints for Regulatory Decision-Making* | Endpoint rationale & construction (§II.A); estimation & missing data (§II.B); meaningfulness — MSD and MSR (§III); design considerations: masking, practice effects, assistive devices, external controls, CAT, burden (§IV.A) |
| **Walton 2015** | Walton et al., *Clinical Outcome Assessments: Conceptual Foundation*, Value Health 18:741–752 (ISPOR TF report 1) | Definitions of treatment benefit, meaningful health aspect, COI (Fig. 1–2), 8 COU components, COA attributes (rater; direct vs indirect; Fig. 3); naming COAs separately from the MAH |
| **Powers 2017** | Powers et al., *ClinRO Assessments of Treatment Benefit*, Value Health 20:2–14 (ISPOR TF report 2) | ClinRO types (readings, ratings, clinician global assessments — CGAs unsuitable as primary); 8 good-measurement practices; continuum of indirectness (Fig. 1); choose the benefit first, not the COI that shows an effect (ABSSSI example); rater reliability |
| **Cano & Hobart 2011** | Cano & Hobart, *The problem with health measurement*, Patient Pref Adherence 5:279–290 | Scaling models; CTT vs Rasch vs IRT; the central problem that scale content, not an explicit construct theory, often defines what a scale measures; case for construct theory tested by predicted item ordering (Stenner) |

## Where the MAH → COI → COA chain is defined
- Treatment benefit = favorable effect on a meaningful aspect of how a patient feels or functions
  in their usual life, or survives (Walton "Treatment Benefit"; G3 §II.B).
- MAH may be narrow (nausea intensity) or broad (lower-limb function); may be hierarchical
  (G3 Fig. 2 — ADLs from hygiene, continence, dressing, feeding, mobility).
- COI = what the COA specifically measures to inform the MAH; identical to the MAH for many PROs,
  a narrower bodily ability for many PerfOs/ClinROs (Walton Fig. 1–2; G3 §II.B.2).
- COU = target population, use of the COA in the endpoint, implementation, trial design, schedule
  (G3 §II.B.3; Walton's 8 components).
- Fit-for-purpose = level of validation sufficient to support the COU (G3 §II.C, from BEST);
  judged by the evidence-based rationale A–H (G3 §IV).
