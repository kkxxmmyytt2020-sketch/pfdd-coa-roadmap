# COA types (G3 App. A–D; Walton attributes; Powers 2017; G1 App. 2 glossary)

Walton's three attributes: (1) does the rating depend on patient volition or rater judgment
(COA vs biomarker); (2) **who** applies judgment — patient (PRO), clinician (ClinRO), non-clinician
observer (ObsRO), or no rater judgment but patient performance (PerfO); (3) **direct** vs **indirect**
measure of the MAH.

## PRO (G3 App. A)
Best when the COI is something known only to the patient (pain, itch, breathlessness), a daily
function or activity, or the impact of symptoms on daily life — **and** the patient can reliably
self-report. Completed by the patient; an aide may record responses without interpreting (e.g.,
fine-motor impairment) under a standardized process. No proxy completion.

## ObsRO (G3 App. B)
Observable signs, events, behaviours reported by a parent/caregiver/other person in daily life,
without medical judgment. For patients who can't self-report (infants, cognitive impairment).
- **Observer items** ("how often did you see your child scratch…?") ✓
- **Proxy items** ("how severe was your child's pain?", "my child felt sad") ✗ — strongly discouraged.
- Predefine minimum contact for an eligible observer; keep the same observer across the trial;
  train alternates; record who reported.

## ClinRO (G3 App. C; Powers 2017)
Clinical judgment needed. Powers' three types:
- **Readings** — dichotomous judgments (fracture present/absent; decision to hospitalize — note
  counts of clinician actions look "hard" but embed variable judgment).
- **Ratings** — ≥ 3 ordered categories or continuous (Ashworth, PASI, lesion area).
- **Clinician global assessments (CGA)** — overall judgments with undefined variables/weights.
  Inadequate as the primary or sole basis for treatment effect (Powers); exploratory "safety net" only.
Implementation: cognitive interviews with clinicians; user manual; rater training, qualification,
refreshers; develop and test as used (photo vs in-person differ); standardized CRF; **intra- and
inter-rater reliability before the registration trial** (ICC, Bland–Altman; prespecify acceptable
variability); masked assessor for primary endpoints; same clinician per patient where feasible.
Most ClinROs are indirect — show the relationship to how patients feel/function (Powers GMP 3).
Split multifaceted items (redness + thickness + scaling in one item) into separate items.

## PerfO (G3 App. D)
Standardized tasks (physical, cognitive, sensory). Advantages: less culture/language variability;
less environmental heterogeneity; no recall error; familiar units (metres, seconds, counts).
Cautions: often indirect → usually pair with a PRO/ObsRO/ClinRO to show meaningfulness; other
functions may interfere (fine motor in a memory test); fatigue/burden and placement in the visit;
follow the manual; equipment availability across sites; **practice effects** (space assessments,
pre-randomization practice, alternate forms — G4 §IV.A.2); record assistive devices and reasons for
non-completion.

## Choosing among types for a COI (component A)
1. Is the COI known only to the patient? → PRO, if self-report is valid in the COU.
2. If self-report isn't valid → what does the COI *look like* to an observer? → ObsRO of behaviours
   (the COI becomes the observable behaviour — say so in Table 1).
3. Does rating require professional judgment of signs? → ClinRO rating (not a CGA).
4. Is the COI a standardized capacity (walking, memory, vision)? → PerfO, and plan component-H
   evidence linking it to daily-life function.
5. Pediatrics (G3 §III.C.3): one version for all ages if valid; otherwise justified multiple
   versions/types; children complete PROs/PerfOs independently; simplified or pictorial response
   scales for young children; confirm vocabulary, concept and recall-period comprehension.
