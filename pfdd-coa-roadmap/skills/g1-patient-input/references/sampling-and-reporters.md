# Sampling methods and reporters (G1 §II.C–D; G2 §VI; G3 App. B)

## Sampling types (G1 Table 2, paraphrased)

| Type | How selected | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple random** | Equal chance for every member of a listed population | Expensive/infeasible; may miss heterogeneity |
| **Stratified random** | Random within mutually exclusive strata (e.g., subtype × sex) | Strata must be known for everyone |
| **Multiplicity** | Probability sample, then members of initial respondents' networks | Useful for rare disease in household surveys; relies on respondents |
| **Cluster / multistage** | Random clusters (sites, states), then people within | Needs cluster sizes; within-cluster homogeneity |
| **Clinical-trial volunteers** | Those who enrol | May not generalize to all patients for the indication |
| **Convenience** | Whoever is available (e.g., PFDD meeting attendees) | Not generalizable |
| **Purposive** | People with specified characteristics | Not generalizable; good for covering heterogeneity |
| **Quota** | Non-random fill of predefined group quotas | Not generalizable, though proportions look right |
| **Respondent-driven / snowball** | Chained referrals | Long chains, unverifiable assumptions; network bias |
| **Web-based** | Online panels, polls | Pre-registered panel limits; response bias |

Probability sampling needs: a well-defined target population, a **sampling frame** listing it
(registries can serve; watch **undercoverage**), and random selection. Non-probability samples can
still be adequate when the objective is to *generate hypotheses or tools* (e.g., concept
elicitation), provided the sample spans the population's heterogeneity (G1 §II.D.2 sense 2).

## Who reports (G1 §II.C.2)
Default: **direct report from patients**, unless they cannot reliably report on the concept.
Factors: level of cognitive development/function or mental status; language; numeracy (needed
for numeric scales); health literacy; health state; comorbidities. Set criteria *before* the study:
to what extent can patients self-report; when are multiple reporters needed; may this change over
the study? Record the reporter for each individual and time point.

Patient- and non-patient reports often disagree markedly on severity and frequency (G1 §II.C.2) —
so a caregiver report is distinct information, not a substitute.

## Observer vs proxy (G2 §VI; G3 App. B)
- **Observer report** — what the caregiver sees the patient do or hears the patient say
  ("how often did you see your child scratching?"). Acceptable; basis of ObsROs.
- **Proxy report** — caregiver reports the patient's inner experience as if they were the patient
  ("how severe was your child's pain?"). FDA strongly discourages it; it can yield inappropriate
  inferences.
- In interviews: caregiver generally not present during the patient interview (or seated behind);
  document presence/assistance; keep sources separable in analysis (G2 §VI).
- Pediatric elicitation: drawing activities, props, shorter segments (G2 §VI).
